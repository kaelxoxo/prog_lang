## Anti-Patterns Verdict

### LLM assessment

Alaga passes the AI-slop test. It does not use gradients, glass, shadows, decorative medical art, generic card grids, or invented controls. The kitchen-table palette, flat tonal hierarchy, explicit patient identity, and plain family-care language feel intentional. The warm-paper surface is justified by the established product identity rather than used as a generic cream default.

The caution is over-composition on empty Home. The screen combines “Next,” a large no-dose message, “All caught up,” a `0 of 0` progress metric, and two actions inside one dominant rounded summary. This is the familiar generated-interface instinct to explain every possible state instead of choosing the one fact the user needs.

## Overall Impression

The app feels calm, responsible, and substantially more trustworthy than a generic medication tracker. Its strongest differentiator is the safety model: the person is named first, consequential changes are reviewable, and uncertainty is described honestly. The biggest opportunity is to make Home equally disciplined: one care truth, one next action, and much less empty-state ceremony.

## What’s Working

1. **Patient identity is a real safety system.** The active person appears before care information and is repeated inside medicine confirmation rather than treated as decoration.
2. **Consequential flows are responsible.** Taken/skipped actions confirm before mutation, archive offers Undo, corrections require a reason, and source wording/history are preserved.
3. **The visual and interaction vocabulary is coherent.** The live dark surface showed flat tonal layers, strong contrast, labeled Lucide icons, and 48 dp controls without gratuitous decoration. Representative contrast pairs meet WCAG AA.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Zero-dose Home communicates false success

**Why it matters:** “All caught up” plus `0 of 0 recorded` can be read as care completed, while “No doses scheduled today” may actually mean that no confirmed medicine exists. A tired caregiver can leave with the wrong conclusion.

**Fix:** When `timeline.length === 0`, replace the entire Next/attention/progress composite with one person-specific truth such as “Lina has no confirmed medicines scheduled today” and one primary action, “Add or manage medicine.” Do not render caught-up or progress language when the denominator is zero.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard` or `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] The patient safety header has become two competing anchors

**Why it matters:** On the live ~407 dp-wide phone, the patient block and adjacent full-height Add tile consume about 99 dp vertically; the relationship already wraps at default font size, and the main task begins well below the top. Large text will make this materially worse.

**Fix:** Keep one compact patient row. Move Add person into the Change-person sheet, or use a secondary row/action that does not narrow the identity text. Test at 1.3×, 1.5×, and 2× system font sizes.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout` and `$impeccable adapt`

### [P1] Android accessibility names and roles are not fully trustworthy

**Why it matters:** The live UI hierarchy announced Home actions with encoded icon glyphs before their labels, despite decorative icons being marked inaccessible. Source review also found a checkbox-shaped permission control announced as a switch and several status views whose labels may not become accessible nodes. Consequential actions must sound clean in TalkBack.

**Fix:** Give every icon-plus-text Pressable an explicit localized `accessibilityLabel`; hide the icon container’s descendants from accessibility; align checkbox/switch visual and semantic roles; make intended status announcements explicit. Physically verify Home, dose actions, medicine rows, patient switching, and sign-out with TalkBack.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### [P2] Large-text and expanded-layout behavior is incomplete

**Why it matters:** The dose-correction bottom sheet is not scrollable despite containing choices, a multiline reason field, the keyboard, and actions. It can overflow on short screens or large type. The global navigation also remains a phone bottom bar on expanded widths, and modal motion does not explicitly honor Reduce Motion.

**Fix:** Make the correction body scrollable with keyboard-safe, always-reachable actions; adapt navigation to a rail/drawer at expanded widths if tablets remain supported; check system Reduce Motion before using slide/fade transitions.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Language and recovery paths do not fully match the supported demo routes

**Why it matters:** Language settings are unreachable before a patient exists because the no-patient branch bypasses Settings, while several exercised management surfaces remain English. Some connection copy assumes shared Wi-Fi even though USB reversal is a supported path, and raw API wording may surface.

**Fix:** Provide Settings/language access in the no-patient state; translate every surface used in the Filipino portfolio route; map technical failures to task-level recovery choices that accurately mention both USB and Wi-Fi local-development paths.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify` and `$impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

### Casey — distracted, one-handed caregiver

The patient/Add block consumes the upper screen and the meaningful empty-state action appears near the bottom of the first viewport. “All caught up” is dangerously glanceable while the qualifying `0 of 0` requires more reading. A rushed caregiver could leave with the wrong conclusion.

### Jordan — first-time medication coordinator

The empty Home screen gives three explanations of zero data and two competing next actions, “Open Today” and “Manage medicines.” It should teach one setup action. There is also no obvious help destination if “confirmed medicine” is unfamiliar.

### Sam — TalkBack and large-text user

The live hierarchy includes encoded glyphs before Home action names, and the relationship field wraps at default size. The source has promising roles, busy/selected states, live regions, and 48 dp targets, but the primary flow cannot be considered accessible until TalkBack and large-text checks physically pass.

## Minor Observations

- The synthetic-data banner is necessary and clear, but it should remain one compact line at large text.
- Expo Go’s floating Tools control creates an apparent second settings gear; remove Expo Go chrome from portfolio captures.
- The dominant pale primary button in dark mode has strong contrast but feels visually detached from the evergreen/clay action system.
- The sign-out row’s full error tint reads like an active warning rather than a routine account action.
- Care Circle begins below the oversized empty summary and is only partially represented near the viewport edge.

## Questions to Consider

- If there are zero doses, what single fact must a tired caregiver trust?
- Should “caught up” ever appear when the denominator is zero?
- Is Add person important enough to permanently consume a full column on every care screen?
- What would Home look like if only the person, the next necessary action, and recorded evidence earned space?
